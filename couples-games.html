Games for Couples: Conversation and Playful Ideas

Playing together is one of the easiest ways to feel close. Here are 33 games for two, from gentle conversation games to silly ones, with simple rules you can explain in a sentence or two. Most need nothing but a pen, a phone or your imagination.

Why games work for couples

Games give you a reason to look up from the screen, to be silly and to learn something new about someone you thought you knew well. They lower the pressure on conversation, because the game does the starting for you. And playing together builds a store of shared jokes and memories.

A few friendly rules
  • Play for fun, not to win. Keep score lightly or not at all.
  • Either of you can pass on any question or round, no explanation needed.
  • If a game starts a real conversation, let it. That is the point.

Conversation games

Calm, curious games that work well at the dinner table, on the sofa or on a walk.

  • Two Truths and a Dream. Each of you shares two true things about yourself and one dream. Guess which is the dream, then talk about it.
  • Would You Rather. Take turns offering two tricky or silly choices. The fun is in the reasons, so always ask why.
  • Rose, Thorn, Bud. Each share a high point, a low point and something you're looking forward to from your day or week.
  • Story Chain. Start a story with one sentence, then take turns adding one sentence each. See where it goes.
  • This or That. Name two things, such as mountains or beach, and each answer at the same time. Count how often you match.
  • Finish My Sentence. One of you starts a sentence like "I feel happiest when..." and the other finishes it however they like, then swap.
  • The Memory Game. Take turns asking about a shared memory. Each of you tells it, then compare the differences.
  • Three Wishes. Each of you gets three wishes for the other person, no money or magic needed, only things that would make their life better.
  • Know Me Quiz. Write ten questions about yourself, such as favourite film or biggest fear, and let your partner answer. Score points kindly.
  • Appreciation Round. Take turns naming one thing you appreciated about the other this week, until you both run out.
  • Hot Seat. One person answers any three questions the other asks, with the right to pass once. Then swap.

Playful games at home

Silly, active or competitive. These need little more than a few minutes and a good mood.

  • Pictionary for Two. One draws a word or phrase on paper, the other guesses. Set a timer for added chaos.
  • Charades. Act out films, songs or everyday moments without speaking. Swap after each round.
  • Draw Each Other. Each draw the other in two minutes without looking at the paper. Compare the results and laugh.
  • Blind Taste Test. One of you blindfolds the other and offers small tastes to guess. Then swap.
  • Cooking Challenge. Each choose a secret ingredient for the other, and cook one dish with it. Judge fairly.
  • Mini Quiz Night. Write five questions each on topics you know well, then quiz each other and keep score.
  • Guess the Song. Play the first few seconds of songs from your phones and guess the title or the memory attached to it.
  • Karaoke Battle. Pick a song each and sing it with full commitment. The loser makes the tea.
  • Compliment Battle. Take turns giving specific compliments. First to run out loses, and both of you win.
  • Photo Scavenger Hunt. Make a list of ten things to find or photograph in your home or on a walk, and race to complete it.
  • Rock, Paper, Scissors for Chores. Settle small decisions, such as who washes up, with a best-of-three. Winner picks the music.

Games to play together or apart

These work in the same room, but also across a text thread, a phone call or a video call, so they suit busy days and different schedules too.

  • 20 Questions. Think of a person, place or object. The other asks up to 20 yes or no questions to guess it.
  • Same Photo Challenge. Pick a theme such as something blue or your breakfast, and each send a photo within five minutes.
  • Watch Along. Pick a film or episode and press play at the same moment on a call, then talk it through afterwards.
  • Song Swap. Each send three songs that mean something to you, with a line about why. Listen to each other's.
  • Emoji Story. Tell a short story using only emojis and see if the other can guess it.
  • Virtual Dinner. Cook the same recipe in two kitchens and eat together on a video call.
  • Question of the Day. Send one question each morning and share answers in the evening, building a small daily habit.
  • Postcard Prompts. Each write three prompts, such as "tell me about your first memory of rain", and send one per day.
  • Online Board Game. Pick a simple board or card game that works online and play on a call. Winner chooses the next one.
  • Guess Where I Am. Send a close-up photo of something nearby, and the other guesses where it is or what it is.
  • Shared Playlist. Build one playlist together, each adding a song that suits the other's mood this week.

Choosing the right game

  • Tired? Pick a conversation game or a shared playlist.
  • Full of energy? Try charades, a cook-off or a karaoke battle.
  • Want to connect? Choose Rose, Thorn, Bud or Know Me Quiz.
  • Short on time? This or That, 20 Questions and Emoji Story take only a few minutes.
  • Not together tonight? Watch Along, Same Photo Challenge or Question of the Day.

Frequently asked questions

What are good games for two people?

Conversation games like Would You Rather and Rose, Thorn, Bud, and playful ones like charades or a mini quiz, all work well with just two people. Choose by mood: calm, silly or curious.

What games can couples play when they are apart?

Ones that need only a phone: 20 Questions, Same Photo Challenge, Emoji Story, a shared playlist, or watching the same film on a call. A daily question also becomes a lovely small habit.

How do we keep games from feeling competitive?

Skip scoring, take turns choosing, and agree that either of you can pass. The aim is to enjoy each other's company, not to win.
