Open When Letter Ideas: 40+ Prompts for Your Partner

Open-when letters are notes written in advance for a particular moment, sealed until the day your partner needs them. They are inexpensive, thoughtful and surprisingly powerful. Here is what they are, 42 prompts to borrow, and how to write ones worth keeping.

What are open when letters?

An open-when letter is a short letter with a condition written on the outside: "Open when you've had a hard day" or "Open when you need to laugh". You write it now, for a moment that will come later. When your partner reaches that moment, they open the letter and find that you thought of them in advance.

People write them for partners who are travelling, starting something stressful, moving house, or simply because it is a lovely thing to do. They work just as well for couples who live together as for couples who spend long stretches apart.

Open when letters for hard moments

These are the letters your partner will be most grateful for. Keep them gentle and honest, and avoid promising that everything will be fine. Often the most comforting thing is to say, "I see you, and I'm on your side."

  • Open when you miss me.
  • Open when you've had a really hard day.
  • Open when you feel anxious or overwhelmed.
  • Open when you can't sleep.
  • Open when you feel like you're not good enough.
  • Open when you're sad and don't know why.
  • Open when you need a laugh.
  • Open when you're feeling stressed about work or study.
  • Open when you need a reminder of how loved you are.
  • Open when you're angry with me.

Open when letters for everyday moments

Small occasions make the best letters. They show that you pay attention to the details of your partner's life.

  • Open when you wake up and need a good start to the day.
  • Open when you're about to do something nerve-racking.
  • Open when you've just achieved something big.
  • Open when you're feeling brave.
  • Open when you're sick and wish someone would look after you.
  • Open when it's raining and you're stuck inside.
  • Open when you're on a long journey.
  • Open when you're having a quiet evening alone.
  • Open when you can't decide what to do.
  • Open when you need a push to rest.

Open when letters about us

These are for remembering, appreciating and looking ahead together. They are a good place for specific memories.

  • Open when you want to remember how we met.
  • Open on the first day of the month.
  • Open when you want to remember our best day.
  • Open when you doubt us.
  • Open when we've had an argument.
  • Open on our anniversary.
  • Open when you want to know what I love about you.
  • Open when you want to plan something for us.
  • Open when you need a reason to smile about our future.
  • Open when you want to know what I'm most grateful for.

Open when letters just because

Light, warm and sometimes funny. A mix of these among the more serious letters keeps the collection from feeling heavy.

  • Open when you've forgotten how funny you are.
  • Open when you want to hear something nice.
  • Open when you're feeling grateful.
  • Open when you've just woken up on a lazy morning.
  • Open when you need a small adventure idea.
  • Open on a random Tuesday.
  • Open when you want to know what I noticed about you today.
  • Open when you're cooking dinner.
  • Open when you want a song recommendation from me.
  • Open when you want to know why I'm proud of you.
  • Open when it's your birthday.
  • Open when you need a hug that I can't give in person.

How to write an open when letter

  1. Imagine the moment. Picture your partner opening it. What would they most like to hear right then?
  2. Be specific. One real memory or detail beats a paragraph of general praise. "I love how you hum when you're cooking" lands better than "you're wonderful".
  3. Keep it short. A few warm sentences are enough. Long letters can feel like homework on a hard day.
  4. Match the tone to the moment. Comfort for a bad day, humour for a dull one, pride for a win.
  5. Add one small thing to do. "Make a cup of tea and take five minutes" or "Play our song" gives the letter a gentle next step.
  6. Write in your own voice. Skip poetry unless it's really how you speak.
  7. Don't overload it with conditions. Avoid anything that sounds like a lecture, a reminder of a mistake, or a request.

Paper or digital?

Paper has a charm that is hard to beat: handwriting, a small envelope, a label on the front. Keep a box where letters can wait. The downside is that a paper letter only helps if your partner has it with them. A digital letter can be read wherever they are, and nobody can peek early by accident.

Frequently asked questions

How many open when letters should I write?

Anywhere from three to ten is a lovely gift. Start with a handful for the moments you know will come, such as a hard day, a big event and a quiet evening, and add more over time.

What do I write if I'm not good with words?

Keep it simple and true. One specific memory, one thing you appreciate, and one sentence about how you feel is enough. Your partner will care much more about sincerity than style.

Can open when letters be funny?

Yes. A mix works best. Funny letters for low-stakes moments, such as a Monday morning, balance out the heartfelt ones, and a laugh can be just as comforting as a kind word.
