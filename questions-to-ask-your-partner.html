100 Questions to Ask Your Partner

Good conversations rarely happen by accident. This list of 100 questions gives you somewhere warm to start, whether you have been together for a month or for twenty years, and whether you are across the table or on opposite sides of town.

How to use this list
  • Pick one or two questions, not ten. Depth beats speed.
  • Answer your own question too. It keeps things fair and makes it a conversation instead of an interview.
  • Listen first. Try repeating back what you heard before you respond.
  • Skip anything that doesn't feel right tonight. You can always come back to it.

Light and fun questions

Easy openers that get you laughing and remind you how much you enjoy each other's company. They work well at dinner, on a walk or while waiting for a bus.

  1. What's a small thing that made you smile this week?
  2. If we had a completely free Saturday, how would you spend it?
  3. What's the best meal you've ever eaten?
  4. Which song instantly puts you in a good mood?
  5. What's a harmless habit of mine that you've secretly come to love?
  6. If you could instantly master any skill, what would it be?
  7. What was your favourite show or cartoon as a kid?
  8. What's the silliest thing you believed as a child?
  9. If we swapped jobs for a day, what would you do first?
  10. Which fictional place would you most like to visit?
  11. What's your comfort food on a bad day?
  12. What's a tiny luxury you'd never give up?
  13. If you could invite anyone to dinner, who would it be?
  14. What's the funniest thing that has happened to you lately?
  15. What does your perfect lazy morning look like?
  16. Which animal do you think you're most like, and why?
  17. What's one food everyone loves that you just don't get?
  18. If you could live in any decade for a month, which would you pick?

Deep questions to ask your partner

These take a little more time. Ask one when you are both relaxed, and be willing to answer it yourself. There are no right answers.

  1. What's something you're proud of that you rarely talk about?
  2. When do you feel most understood by me?
  3. What's a worry you've never said out loud?
  4. What did you need more of when you were growing up?
  5. How do you like to be comforted when you're upset?
  6. What's a moment that changed how you see yourself?
  7. What do you wish people asked you more often?
  8. Is there something you're still working through that I could support you with?
  9. What does feeling safe with someone mean to you?
  10. When do you feel most like yourself?
  11. What's a lesson you learned the hard way?
  12. What part of yourself are you still learning to accept?
  13. How do you know when you need space, and how can I make that easier for you?
  14. What does success mean to you, apart from money?
  15. What's a belief you've changed your mind about?
  16. What's something you could forgive easily, and something you'd find hard to forgive?
  17. What's the kindest thing anyone has ever done for you?
  18. When did you last feel truly at peace?

Questions about the future

Talking about what you each want, out loud and without pressure, makes it easier to build something that works for both of you.

  1. Where do you picture us living in five years?
  2. What would a good ordinary day look like for us in ten years?
  3. Is there a place you'd like us to live in for a while, just once?
  4. What's something you'd like us to learn together?
  5. How do you feel about having children, and has that view changed over time?
  6. What does a healthy balance between work and rest look like to you?
  7. What's one big goal you'd like to reach this year, and how can I help?
  8. How do you imagine we'll handle money as our lives grow?
  9. Which traditions would you like us to build or keep?
  10. What do you want our home to feel like?
  11. What adventure is still on your list?
  12. What do you hope we're like when we're old?
  13. What would you do with a year if money and time were no concern?
  14. What's a risk you'd like us to take together?
  15. Which of your dreams do you worry is too big to say out loud?
  16. What would make you say, ten years from now, that we did this well?

Questions about us

A chance to say the good things that often stay unsaid, and to check in on how the relationship feels from the inside.

  1. What's your favourite memory of us?
  2. What's something I do that makes you feel loved?
  3. What's one thing we do well as a team?
  4. What's something you'd like us to do more often?
  5. When did you first know you wanted me around?
  6. What's our funniest in-joke, and where did it come from?
  7. How could I support you better when you're stressed?
  8. Is there anything you've been meaning to tell me but haven't found the moment?
  9. What's a small habit of ours you'd hate to lose?
  10. How do you feel about the way we handle disagreements?
  11. What do you wish we had more time for?
  12. What's something I've taught you, even by accident?
  13. When did you last feel really close to me?
  14. What do you think each of us brings to this relationship that the other doesn't?
  15. What does a perfect evening together look like to you?
  16. How do you like to be told that you're appreciated?

Questions for new couples

Getting to know someone is a slow, enjoyable process. Keep these curious and low-pressure, and skip any that don't feel right yet.

  1. What does a typical weekend look like for you?
  2. What are you most passionate about?
  3. How do you like to balance time with friends and time alone?
  4. What's your family like, and how close are you to them?
  5. What do you value most in a friendship?
  6. Do you prefer to talk by text, by phone or in person?
  7. What are you looking for right now?
  8. How do you like to give and receive affection?
  9. What are three things that matter most to you in a relationship?
  10. What would your dream first trip together look like?
  11. How do you tend to handle conflict when you're upset?
  12. What's a quirk of yours I should know about early on?
  13. What are you most curious about when it comes to me?
  14. If you're comfortable sharing, what did you learn from your past relationships?
  15. How do you think about work and ambition?
  16. What helps you feel relaxed around someone new?

Questions for long-term couples

After years together it is easy to assume you already know every answer. These are designed to show you what has quietly changed.

  1. What have you changed your mind about since we got together?
  2. How have we each changed over the last few years?
  3. What do you miss about the early days, and what do you love more now?
  4. Which of our routines would you like to refresh?
  5. When do you feel most like partners rather than housemates?
  6. What's one small thing I could do to make your week easier?
  7. What have you always wondered about me but never asked?
  8. Is there an unspoken expectation we should say out loud?
  9. How do you feel about how we share chores and responsibilities?
  10. What's something new we haven't tried yet?
  11. What do you appreciate about me that you haven't said lately?
  12. What's the best decision we've made together?
  13. What would you like us to protect, no matter how busy life gets?
  14. How can we make more time for each other?
  15. Which hard stretch are you proudest that we got through?
  16. What would you tell the couple we were at the start?

Tips for a good conversation

  • Choose a calm moment. A slow evening, a long drive or a Sunday morning works better than the middle of a stressful week.
  • Put phones away if you are together, or give each other your full attention on a call if you are not.
  • Follow the curiosity. If an answer surprises you, ask "tell me more" instead of moving to the next question.
  • Avoid keeping score. These questions are for understanding each other, not for winning a point later.
  • Come back to it. Pick a regular slot, such as one question every Sunday, and the habit will do more than any single deep talk.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions should we ask in one sitting?

Two or three is plenty. A single question that leads to a real conversation is worth more than working through a long list quickly.

What if a question makes my partner uncomfortable?

Let it go without pressure. Say that it is fine to skip it, and move on to something lighter. Trust grows when people know they can say no.

What's the best time to ask these questions?

Whenever you are both relaxed and not rushed: over dinner, on a walk, before sleep, or on a call. Avoid starting a deep question when one of you is tired or stressed.
